# Restoration after a Board A USB dropout

When a drive helper cannot verify restoration, its wildcard inhibit protects **every** channel. The broker also remembers the failure in memory. Do not end an inhibit to get telemetry back without establishing why it was written. `--enable-usb-drop-recovery` is a narrow exception (in the installed broker unit since the owner-approved activation of 2026-11-23; the flag is the off switch, see Rollback): the broker that saw the grant, armed status and failed restoration can verify replacement devices and end its own records. There is no automatic USB reset, diagnostic transmission, routine resumption or helper launch in this transaction. Normal helper admission still requires new qualifying evidence.

## What the evidence establishes

The collector has 900 seconds from the first latch. Its C-CAN helper must have latched; B-CAN alone does not qualify. For each latched helper it must have the same Board A kernel instance before the grant and after arming. Those ifindexes must be gone from a complete netdev inventory. Both current roles must resolve by serial and controller number to a *different USB enumeration* of Board A, with both replacement links positively DOWN. A rename, a reset with the same USB device number, missed packets, a USB monitor event or a successful read by itself is not enough. USB monitor IDs in the record are correlation only.

The broker refuses if a radar service-alignment run was requested during the C-CAN launch, was active at its claim or is active now, or if this history is uncertain. It waits for both claims and the auxiliary thread to finish, no hold, no unrecorded startup fault, a quiet broker service cgroup, and no foreign same-boot inhibit. An unverifiable cgroup, a missing or altered owned record, or a boot/time mismatch cannot authorize a recovery. Nonparticipating processes outside the service are not ruled out by these checks.

The collector pauses both display observers and locks all roles in order: C-CAN and B-CAN exclusively, CAN CH and spare shared. It resolves and locks the current Board A channel names exclusively, then repeats its checks. Shared Board B observers may continue. No Board B channel is configured. Before configuring anything it spends **one attempt per boot**, including unsuccessful attempts: the exclusive-create reservation is file- and directory-synced. The broker never resumes an incident opened by another process.

Before it pauses a display or takes a lock, the collector checks that `/home/pi/hooks/ignition_is_on` is a regular file. It checks again under the locks before spending the attempt. Missing, wrong-type or inaccessible means `waiting/ignition_not_on` until the window expires. The producer is `ignitionmon.service` (`/home/pi/scripts/ignition_monitor.sh`, MacBook-managed). Its Wi-Fi scans detect an ignition-associated network; the script creates the marker on a positive scan and removes it after three negative scans. That is a scan count, not a time limit. **A present marker is never engine evidence.** Key-on without the engine running can set it; a stopped or stalled monitor can leave its last positive marker indefinitely. There is no live service probe or age check. If the monitor stops with the marker absent, recovery cannot fire. If it stops with the marker present, the ordinary C-CAN bootstrap can still spend the attempt: a silent bus fails identity (`wrong_bus`); a recognizable bus without running RPM fails `engine_not_running`. Neither case ends an inhibit or commands B-CAN.

First it starts C-CAN in classical listen-only mode. One kernel request addresses the numeric ifindex directly (no name or alternative name), setting bit timing and passive flags together with UP. Fresh readback must match the expected instance, bitrate, listen-only, FD off, one-shot off, restart-ms 0 and ERROR-ACTIVE. The collector passively identifies C-CAN and requires three fresh RPM samples at or above 400. Only then does it configure and verify B-CAN the same way and refresh both topology records. After rechecking gates, processes, inhibits and both passive links, it samples running RPM again. Immediately before each removal the broker checks the engine sample's age and refuses to issue the removal if it is over 2 s. That is a check, not a bound on completion: scheduling or I/O (a blocked journal, a descheduled process) can delay the removal after the check, and that can be neither prevented nor retracted, because readers act on the record's absence, so nothing re-creates a removed record. The clock is read after each unlink and a removal found finished after the deadline is detected and reported late; this cannot distinguish a late issue from a late completion. A late last or only removal ends as `committed_late` (inhibits ended, every latch kept, an error-level log line, no rollback; the dashboard keeps the restoration fault until a person restarts the broker). A late first removal of two ends as `partial_commit` with the late flag in the incident detail and the second record untouched. If no record was removed, an expired deadline permits one fresh sample and retry.

Each identity or RPM observation runs in its own unprivileged `usb_recovery_probe` child in the broker's service cgroup. The collector never opens a CAN socket; in particular it need not wait for socket creation, filter setup, bind or close to leave the kernel. It brackets the reads with instance readbacks as before. The collector bounds the whole child, including interpreter startup, around the 0.25 s identity or 0.5 s RPM acquisition. The initial identity and up to three RPM children share the bootstrap deadline below; the separate commit-time RPM child has its own five-second bound. On expiry the collector sends SIGKILL and waits at most two seconds to reap that PID. A valid reply is exactly one JSON line of at most 512 bytes, with a boolean result and ordered monotonic start and finish within the parent's spawn/receipt interval. The final RPM deadline uses the child's **start**, not its finish or the parent's receipt. A failed child or invalid reply aborts the attempt (`wrong_bus` or `engine_not_running`). A child still unreaped after SIGKILL is named by PID in the incident and logged; neither this receive-only child nor its unprivileged read-only `ip` counter reads can submit link changes, so the cgroup offender classes do not change.

**Bootstrap and accepted physical limit.** The engine cannot be proven running with Board A DOWN. With the ignition flag present but the engine not running, expect roughly two to four seconds of listen-only observation in normal operation. There is **one five-second evidence budget**, measured on the monotonic clock immediately before submission of the C-CAN UP command. The setter wait, its readback, the bracketing link reads and all initial probe children use the remaining time; none gets a fresh five seconds. If time runs out no further probe starts, no B-CAN command is sent, and the attempt is spent. With prompt cleanup and safety checks, the collector submits DOWN by five seconds, plus the time for DOWN itself. Five seconds is **not** a hard physical UP-time or unconditional DOWN-submission guarantee: reaping a stuck probe can add two seconds, and rollback still needs positive submitter quiescence and fresh instance checks. A descheduled broker, stuck setter or slow kernel or filesystem can delay submission; if rollback cannot be verified, or the broker dies, the link can remain UP with the inhibits in place until inspected. Enabling this bootstrap requires the owner's explicit acceptance. The owner accepted the separate unbenchmarked firmware risk: a new kernel netdev, a changed USB devnum and host listen-only readback do not physically prove an already-powered controller stopped transmitting across a disconnect. The consequence is lower while driving, when diagnostics already use a transmit-capable adapter at the right bitrate; when parked, a stuck controller can keep modules awake and drain the battery.

## Commit, refusal and crash recovery

The expected owned names, in order, are `vehicle-data-restoration-failed` and, only if its helper latched, `vehicle-data-bcan-restoration-failed`. The end operation compares *all* same-boot records to the exact expected payloads under the inhibit writer lock, checks the clock before and after each unlink and syncs the directory after each. A concurrent writer unable to obtain the lock publishes a distinct wildcard `nolock.<boot>.<name-hash>`; its `stands_for` identifies the logical name. Both current and older readers honour the wildcard. No conditional end removes a fallback. A late fallback survives even if it arrives during a commit.

Run inhibit tools as the service user `pi`, never with `sudo`. A root-created `.inhibit-writers.lock` (mode `0640`) makes subsequent `pi` writers fall back and manual ends refuse even with no lock holder; restore ownership without deleting or replacing the lock: `sudo chown pi:pi -- /home/pi/dev/obd-things/tmp/locks/operation-state/.inhibit-writers.lock`.

Before any record has been removed, failure means leave every inhibit and latch in place and attempt rollback for every possibly effective mutation, including one that timed out. A preflight refusal is `not_submitted`: rollback never takes down a link recovery did not attempt to bring up. A confirmed kernel rejection likewise needs no rollback. A quiet cgroup must be positively established before trusting rollback: a root submitter can outlive its `sudo` process. A replacement whose DOWN state or disappearance cannot be proved is `rollback_unverified`; inspect it. After any removal there is **no rollback**: the links were verified passive at the commit. A partial commit leaves both in-memory latches; a remaining wildcard protects every channel. Only a complete, on-time commit (`committed`) clears the incident's in-memory latches. A directory-sync failure after unlink is recorded; power loss could restore the inhibit, which is conservative. A crash before reservation leaves the old wildcards for a person. A crash after reservation leaves that budget spent, with zero, one or both replacement links possibly UP listen-only. A crash after the first unlink leaves the second wildcard. Broker death loses the in-memory latches and may leave only an incident stuck in `committing`, a spent reservation and no inhibit records, with no durable `committed_late` warning: after the last unlink other processes see no restoration inhibit. Treat a `committing` incident as an unknown outcome and inspect the remaining inhibits. At startup with the feature enabled, one error-level journal line reports this boot's `committing` incident (`committing` is written before the final RPM sample, so it does not prove any unlink was attempted) and lists the inhibit files on disk, restoration names first and filenames percent-encoded. If the list exceeds one journal line it ends with the number omitted (`none` for an empty list, `inventory unavailable` on a listing error). The diagnostic attempts, resumes and changes nothing, and a failure to produce it never affects startup. A restart otherwise follows ordinary startup assessment.

`journalctl -u van-telemetry` reports transitions. Journal output is best-effort: the recovery's log calls only enqueue into a bounded queue (64 lines) that one daemon writer thread (`usb_recovery_log.py`, started only when the feature is enabled) writes, so a stalled journal pipe can never hold a rollback, a lease or a display pause. When the queue is full a line is dropped and counted, and the next delivered line says how many were lost; stdout's blocking mode is left alone. At startup the default sink duplicates stdout’s descriptor; the writer then uses `os.write`, not Python’s buffered stdout. Each line, including its newline, fits one atomic pipe write (up to `PIPE_BUF` bytes), so a blocked journal writer cannot hold the buffered-stream lock at process exit. The incident file, not the journal, is the durable trail. A sink is only ever the writer thread's target, never called from the recovery's own thread. Journal loss (a dropped line, a raising sink, a writer thread that could not start) never changes an outcome or invalidates evidence: only the durable incident-file write decides whether a capture succeeded. A raising sink loses its line and the drop count it would have carried goes to the next delivered line. The incident JSON files and `reservation-<boot_id>.json` are under `tmp/vehicle_data/usb-recovery/`; they are audit evidence, not recovery instructions. The vocabulary and exact limits live in `usb_recovery_contract.py`: `open` and `waiting` precede `reserved`, `configuring`, `verifying`, `committing`. Terminal states include `ineligible`, `failed`, `committed`, `partial_commit`, `committed_late`, `committed_latches_kept` and `commit_outcome_unknown`. Inspect the state's `reason`, submitted mutations, rollback outcomes, actual ended names and durability. At most eight incidents per boot, eight latch entries per helper and 16 KiB per incident are retained; the current boot is never pruned, even across clock corrections. Seven older boots are retained. There is no new dashboard/API status field.

## Install, verification and activation

Status: activated 2026-11-23 (owner acceptance of the bootstrap and of the late-removal limit recorded in the workstream; the unit carries the flag). Verified that day: whole offline suite; flag-off parked restart with no recovery line and no record directory; link program installed with digest verified; the unprivileged request refused by the kernel (`EPERM`); the privileged request only in throwaway namespaces; the activation restart with the `usb-drop-recovery enabled` journal line, all three vehicle roles healthy and listen-only and TX counters unchanged. Not verified: a real mid-drive recovery, a real USB disconnect, adapter firmware behaviour. The steps below are the procedure used and the one to repeat after a link-program change (a change to `usb_recovery_link/` or to `LINK_HELPER_SHA256` takes effect only through `install.sh install`). They do not by themselves authorize an install, a restart, a CAN request or fault injection.

1. For a first deployment, merge the reviewed code **without the separate activation commit**. With the van parked/asleep follow the [guarded broker restart procedure](../README.md#restarting-the-broker). Confirm this startup has no `usb-drop-recovery` line and (on first deployment) the record directory does not exist.
2. Run `projects/vehicle_data/usb_recovery_link/install.sh install` and `projects/vehicle_data/usb_recovery_link/install.sh status`. Status must verify the reviewed digest and installed owner/mode. Root runs only `/usr/local/sbin/vanpi-can-link-by-index`, not checkout code. Check the role link states and TX counters before and after the following operator checks; they must not change.
3. The live-namespace privilege check only lists authorization. Never send a privileged request in that namespace to a guessed unused index. Run the following from a shell as the service user; the first check must list the command as allowed. The second is **unprivileged** and must exit 3 with `errno=1 EPERM`.

   ```sh
   helper=/usr/local/sbin/vanpi-can-link-by-index
   deadline() { python3 -c 'import time; print(time.monotonic() + 2)'; }
   sudo -n -l -- /usr/bin/python3 -I -B "$helper" down 99999 --not-after "$(deadline)"
   /usr/bin/python3 -I -B "$helper" down 99999 --not-after "$(deadline)"
   ```

4. Test privileged acknowledgements **only in disposable network namespaces**, with no vehicle interface present. In order, expect exit 0 (namespace loopback), exit 3 `EOPNOTSUPP` (not CAN), exit 3 `ENODEV` (absent there).

   ```sh
   sudo -n unshare --net /usr/bin/python3 -I -B "$helper" down 1 --not-after "$(deadline)"
   sudo -n unshare --net /usr/bin/python3 -I -B "$helper" up-listen-only 1 500000 --not-after "$(deadline)"
   sudo -n unshare --net /usr/bin/python3 -I -B "$helper" down 7 --not-after "$(deadline)"
   ```

5. (Done 2026-11-23.) Only after review, successful inactive checks **and explicit owner acceptance of the bootstrap**, apply the separate `Activation:` unit change, install the tracked unit, reload systemd and run the guarded parked restart. Verify `systemctl show -p ExecStart van-telemetry.service` contains the flag and the journal for that startup says `usb-drop-recovery enabled`. A missing, improperly owned or changed helper disables recovery. The helpers and grant pipe protocol are unchanged. The installed state or a parked restart does not prove an armed mid-drive recovery. An optional physical unplug/replug test at idle with the vehicle parked and helpers armed requires separate owner authorization. `usbreset` does not reproduce a fresh enumeration.

## Rollback

1. Inspect every resolved channel with `python3 -m tools.can_operation_state status --channel <ch>` and inspect any `tmp/locks/operation-state/inhibit-nolock.*.json`. **Any active inhibit blocks a guarded restart**, including a fallback. Resolve its recorded condition under that operation's own inspection/end procedure. Do not end it merely to roll back or bypass the restart guard. An older CLI can list, honour and end the fallback by its own name.
2. Remove the activation flag, reinstall the unit, reload systemd and run the guarded parked restart. Confirm there is no enabled line for that startup. Recovery files are inert; preserve them.
3. For a code rollback, before reverting the source run `projects/vehicle_data/usb_recovery_link/install.sh uninstall`. Its `status` must now report absent. Then revert the code and perform the guarded parked restart. There is no need to restart unrelated services or delete audit records.
